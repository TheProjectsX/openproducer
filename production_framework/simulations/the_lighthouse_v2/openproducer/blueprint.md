## Section 2: Raw Plot Archive (Historical Record Only)

> *Notice: This section preserves the verbatim raw plot originally provided by the creator. It serves strictly as an archival record for future reference and is not parsed during standard production passes.*

```text
You have been alone on this barren rock in the Atlantic Ocean for an entire month.

No visitors. No escape from the endless crash of the surf. You are the sole lighthouse keeper, bound to a single duty: keep the beam burning so passing ships survive the dark.

Until tonight.

A violent gale hammers the stone tower. You finish trimming the lamp wick and start down the spiral iron staircase.

Then, you freeze.

Above you, on the walkway you just left, heavy footsteps pace the floorboards.

Slow. Deliberate.

You raise your lantern and climb back up. The room is empty—except for a storm hatch standing wide open to the driving rain. You know you latched it minutes ago. You slam it shut, lock it tight, and head back down.

Instantly, the pacing starts again. Directly over your head. The ceiling timbers creak under unmistakable weight.

Down in your quarters, you try to steady your breath. Then comes a sound that makes your blood run cold: three sharp knocks on the front door.

Nobody lands a boat in seas like this.

You take your lantern to the entrance and pull back the heavy iron bolt. You swing the door open into the howling gale. Nothing. Just churning, black water.

Then your light catches the stone threshold.

Wet footprints. Bare human feet, soaked in brine, leading straight from the surf up to your boots. None lead back out.

You slam the door shut and back away.

Right behind your ear, a familiar voice whispers your name. It belongs to someone you buried years ago.

You bolt to the radio desk, flicking the transmitter frantically. "Mayday, Mayday. Lighthouse station, does anyone read?"

Static hisses. Then a calm voice answers through the speaker:

"Extinguish the light. It sees you."

You turn slowly toward the seaward window. Out among the cresting waves stands a dark silhouette, perfectly motionless on the churning water.

You blink—and it vanishes.

Suddenly, the main lighthouse beam dies. Pitch black.

The backup generator kicks in, and in the glass pane before you, you see a pale face reflected right over your shoulder.

You whip around, swinging blindly. The room is empty.

Panic takes over. You run for the exit, tearing the door open. But the gale has died into an unnatural, suffocating fog.

A voice whispers from the white mist: "What took you so long?"

Before you can move, breath hits the side of your neck: "You were never alone."

Every bulb in the tower shatters. In the blackness, heavy glass crashes on the top floor.

You strike a match, relight your lantern, and mount the spiral stairs. With every step you take, a second set of footsteps follows directly behind you.

*Clang.*

You reach the upper doorway. The rusted door slowly swings open into the dark.

From inside the gloom, a voice speaks. Smooth. Calm.

It is your own voice.

"Come inside."

You are frozen in the hallway, listening to your own mouth speak from the dark.

Your lantern flame abruptly snuffs out. A sharp gasp cuts the silence—then a scream that rattles the iron frame of the tower.

Then, absolute stillness.

Days later, a relief tender arrives under clear skies.

They find the lighthouse cold and abandoned. The kettle sits on the stove. The radio is off. Your lantern lies shattered at the base of the stairs.

They search the cliffs, the rocks, the shoreline. No trace of a body.

Then the crew climbs to the top storeroom.

The storm window is bolted tight from the inside. And across the dusty floor runs a trail of damp seawater footprints. They begin out of nowhere in the middle of the room, lead straight to the glass, and stop.

No tracks coming in. No tracks leaving.

Some claim the solitude broke you and you jumped. But that will never explain a window locked from the inside.

And it will never explain the voice that answered the radio.

The lighthouse still stands. Tonight, you can almost hear three sharp knocks against the glass, eighty feet above the sea.

The radio clicks on by itself.

And your own voice whispers:

"It's your turn to keep the light burning."
```

## Section 3: Structured Story, Subjects & Environments Plan

### 3.1 Primary Subject DNA (The Lighthouse Keeper)
* **Form & Materiality:** Pure unbroken solid black vector stroke silhouette (`#0F172A`) terminating in smooth rounded line-cap ends (`stroke-linecap: round`). Strictly zero fingers, zero knuckles, zero toes.
* **Head Standard:** Perfect circular head filled with solid opaque white (`#FFFFFF`) with a clean black contour (`#0F172A`), completely blank without facial features.
* **Costume / Silhouette Anchor:** Distinguishable lighthouse keeper silhouette via a wide-brimmed oilskin sou'wester hat silhouette and long heavy keeper coat hem.
* **Prop Anchor:** A vintage brass hurricane lantern casting a warm, motivated conical vector glow onto the surrounding canvas.
* **Doppelgänger / Entity Variant:** Identical stick figure silhouette emerging from deep shadow, representing the uncanny double whose voice mirrors the keeper.

### 3.2 Environment DNA (The Atlantic Lighthouse Tower)
* **Master Setting:** Weather-beaten stone lighthouse tower perched on an isolated Atlantic rock surrounded by turbulent dark waters.
* **Architectural Staging & Planes:**
  * *Foreground / Midground:* Iron spiral staircase with clean diagonal geometric railing lines; stone arched entrance door with heavy sliding bolt; cramped radio desk with vacuum-tube transceiver; upper lantern room with large faceted Fresnel lens ring and storm hatch.
  * *Background:* Pitch-black Atlantic ocean, driving horizontal rain lines, dense unnatural white sea mist during supernatural lulls.
* **Lighting Vectors:** Stark, high-contrast minimalist noir lighting. A single dominant directional beam or warm lantern glow (`#FFE8A3`) falling off sharply into impenetrable pitch-black shadow (`#0A0B0E`) across a warm textured paper canvas (`#F8F6F0`).
